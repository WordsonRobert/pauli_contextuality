# 05. Three qubits

## TQ-1 — `deg_grid`, `deg_doily`, `deg_W5`
1. Peres–Mermin square: a set of rows and columns covering each point an even number of times contains row i iff it
   contains column j, for all i, j. So the cycle space is {∅, all six lines}. Its parity is 1 (certificate D2), hence
   Im H is the even-weight code and deg = distance from an odd-weight vector = 1.
2. Doily W(3,2): CERT-1b gives ≥ 3. The all-zero assignment violates exactly the 3 negative lines (D1). Alternative:
   exhaustive search over 2^15 assignments (`native_decide`).
3. W(5,2): TR-1' with the family of all 336 doilies (each of the 315 lines lies in the same number of them; check by
   computation, or by Sp(6,2)-transitivity) gives ≥ 315·3/15 = 63. The explicit witness (certificates/witness_W5_2.txt)
   violates 63.

## TQ-2 (universal 1/5 bound) — `three_qubit_bound`
Every configuration S on ≤ 3 qubits satisfies deg S ≤ ⌊|L(S)|/5⌋.
**Steps.**
1. Pad to 3 qubits (ALG-3). S is then a sub-configuration of W(5,2).
2. Apply TR-1 (Sp(6,2) line-transitive) with G = W(5,2): ρ(S) ≤ ρ(W(5,2)) = 63/315 = 1/5.
   **Group-free alternative.** Let F = (gS)_{g∈Sp(6,2)}, or any family of copies of S with uniform coverage of W(5,2).
   Then 63 = deg W(5,2) ≥ |F|·deg S/c with c|L(W5)| = |F||L(S)|, so deg S/|L(S)| ≤ 63/315. Formally you need a
   uniform family of copies of an *arbitrary* S. This is where the group (Sp(6,2) as a finite set of 6×6 matrices,
   or generated by transvections) is used. Lean: define Sp(6,2) as a `Finset` of matrices by enumeration (1451520
   elements: heavy), or prove transitivity on lines via explicit transvections.
3. Equality cases: doily (3/15), W(5,2), Q⁺(5,2) (21/105) and Q⁻(5,2) (9/45). Lower bounds for the quadrics: a parabolic
   section Q(4,2) ≅ doily via projection from the radical vector a (q(p+a) = q(p)+1 makes the projection bijective
   on singular points) together with line-transitivity of O(q). Upper bounds: restrict the W(5,2) witness (R1).

## TQ-3 (rigidity) — `W5_rigid`
Every optimal assignment of W(5,2) violates exactly 3 lines of each of its 336 doilies, 21 of each of its 36 Q⁺(5,2)
and 9 of each of its 28 Q⁻(5,2). This is TR-2 with all ratios equal to 1/5.
